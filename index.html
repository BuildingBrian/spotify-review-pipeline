01 The problem

No team can read 660,622 reviews.

That is how many public Google Play reviews the Spotify app collected from May 2022 to November 2023. Star ratings do not say what to fix: most reviews are either five stars or one star.

The useful detail is in what people wrote, and there is too much of it to read. Without a way to read it all, a team hears the loudest complaint, not the most common or the most serious one.

89,104reviews arrived in October 2023 alone, the busiest month
45.3% and 36.7%are five-star and one-star reviews. A rating shows the mood, not the cause.

02 How it works

Code runs the process. AI only reads the language.

Six stages, each with one job. AI does the reading and the writing. Plain code does the counting, the ranking, and the checking, so the numbers do not depend on an AI getting arithmetic right.

  1. Prepare

    Code

    Loads all 660,622 reviews and draws a random sample of 150,000 to analyze.

  2. Label

    AI

    Reads each review and tags its topic, its intent, and how severe the problem is, with an exact quote as proof.

  3. Verify

    AI

    A second AI pass re-labels 2,310 reviews without seeing the first answers. Code compares the two.

  4. Group

    Code AI

    Code sorts the complaints into 45 issues. AI only writes a plain name for each one.

  5. Rank

    Code

    Scores each issue by how many people raised it and how severe it is.

  6. Recommend

    AI Code

    AI drafts the recommendation from the ranked numbers. Code re-checks every figure it cites.

Code fixed rules, same answer every runAI a language model reading or writing text

Technical detail for engineers
  • Four separate AI roles, each with its own prompt: label, verify, name issues, write the memo. Labeling, verification, and naming use Claude Haiku 5.5. The memo uses Claude Sonnet 5.5. See prompts/.
  • At most 50 reviews per request. Code validates every answer: labels must come from a fixed list, and the quote must match the review word for word. A bad answer is retried once, then set aside with a reason.
  • State is saved after every batch. The real run was interrupted at 23,096 reviews and resumed without re-sending a single completed one.
  • 34,459 reviews had text identical to another review and reused its validated labels.
  • The ranking can be rebuilt from saved files with one command and no AI calls: python -m pipeline rerank.
  • Python, SQLite for run state, FastAPI and Postgres for the dashboard. Code: pipeline/. Label definitions: labels/definitions.md. Full write-up: README.

03 What users said

Two issues make up 26.0% of all complaints.

Of 149,972 classified reviews, 67,377 (44.9%) were complaints or said the writer was leaving. The pipeline sorted those into 45 issues. Free-plan limits and ads lead every other specific issue by a wide margin.

04 Recommendations

Start with the free plan.

Three priorities, ordered by a simple score: how many people raised the issue times how severe it is. Each one starts from the user's problem, then the evidence, then who it reaches.

1 Priority 1 · Main investment

Start with free‑plan restrictions

People on the free plan say basic controls are locked behind Premium: limited skips, forced shuffle, and no way to pick a specific song.

3 Priority 3 · Targeted fix

Fix login failures

People cannot get past the login screen, or keep getting logged out, even with the right password.

1,877complaints
2.8%of all complaints

Evidence
The most severe issue in the data. 1,725 of 1,877 (91.9%) were rated 4 or 5, meaning a core task is blocked.
Who it reaches
A small group, but one that cannot use the product at all. A focused repair, not the main investment.

Considered and not prioritized. Playback that stops or plays the wrong song drew 2,200 complaints at an average severity of 3.26. It is severe, but it reaches far fewer people than the free-plan issue. The largest group of all, 20,437 complaints of general dissatisfaction, names no specific problem, so there is nothing to act on.

Priority scores: 26,088, 17,269, 7,340, and 7,169 for playback. Severity runs from 1 to 5: a 3 means a feature is restricted but usable, a 4 means a core task is blocked. These priorities say where to look first. The data has no revenue, plan, or churn figures, so I did not estimate business impact.

05 How I checked the work

Three checks, with the weak spots left in.

The labels come from AI, so I tested them against a second AI pass and against my own hand labels. Neither check makes the labels ground truth.

How often the labels agreed

Agreement with the pipeline's labels, by field
FieldSecond AI pass, 2,310 reviewsMy hand labels, 50 reviews
Topic87.0%54%
Intent93.3%90%
Severity86.2%72%

Second AI pass. A separate prompt re-labeled a random 2% sample without seeing the first answers. It uses the same AI model, so it measures consistency, not truth.

My hand labels. I labeled 50 reviews before seeing any AI output. Afterward I reviewed the 28 reviews where we disagreed: 16 were my own labels breaking the written definitions, 8 were ambiguous, and 4 were AI errors. I did that review after seeing the AI's answers, so I report it separately.

Every label needs an exact quote

Code rejects a label unless its quote matches the review word for word. 41 of 150,013 reviews were set aside: 13 were empty and 28 had quotes that did not match.

Every number is recomputed

Code recomputed all 9 figures cited in the recommendation memo from the saved data. All 9 matched.

Tested with hostile input

8 of 8 written test reviews were labeled correctly, including two that ordered the AI to change its labels.

What this cannot tell you

  • These are public reviews from people who chose to write one, between May 2022 and November 2023. They are not a sample of all users.
  • “I am leaving” is what people wrote, not what they did. The data has no revenue, plan, or churn figures.
  • My own topic labels matched the AI's only 54% of the time, and 50 reviews is a small test.
  • The second AI pass checked topic, intent, and severity. It did not check the finer issue label that the ranking uses.
  • The largest group, 20,437 complaints (30.3%), names no specific problem. How reviews split between that group and the specific issues is the least certain part.
